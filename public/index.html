VIDEO STUDY

Simple marks. Intricate patterns.

The reel shows six designs made on latte foam with piped chocolate and a single fine metal skewer. The artwork comes from moving what is already on the surface: dots stretch into lobes, loops become pointed leaves, and repeated crossings create scallops.

These are procedural approximations of the six designs. The video uses an oblique camera and cuts; ingredient ratios, tool depth and exact stroke paths cannot be measured from it. Recipe names here are descriptive, not the artist’s titles.

How this studio works

Piping deposits chocolate into a 512 × 512 material field. Each skewer movement locally warps both chocolate and the pale foam, using bilinear sampling from a snapshot of the previous surface. Small spatial steps keep a fast and a slow gesture comparable. An optical-density curve keeps diluted chocolate visible. The model preserves the visible cause and effect without pretending to solve fluid chemistry.

A soft drag shoulder makes rounded bends; a narrower pull near the center keeps the pale channels open. Replay applies the same fixed spatial samples progressively, so changing its speed preserves the final pattern. Undo stores complete gestures; all six recipes use the same tools as freehand drawing.
